Introduction

What is Robotics?

Robotics is the field of science and technology that studies the design, construction, programming, and operation of robots. It brings together three major areas of knowledge: mechanics, electronics, and programming.

A robot is a machine capable of capturing information from the environment (sensors), processing that data (computer/controller), and performing physical actions (actuators). Some robots can learn and adapt using Artificial Intelligence.

Definition: According to the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE), a robot is a mechanical, electrical, or software system capable of performing tasks autonomously or semi-autonomously.

Relevance

Why is Robotics Important?

Robotics is revolutionizing the way we live, work, and interact with technology.

Productivity

Robots can work 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, without breaks, significantly increasing production in factories and businesses worldwide.

Safety

Robots take over dangerous tasks such as handling toxic substances, mine operations, bomb disposal, and space exploration.

Innovation

Robotics drives advances in medicine, agriculture, education, and space exploration, creating solutions that did not previously exist.

Fun Facts

Did You Know?

Interesting facts about the world of robotics that most people are unaware of.

Fun Fact 1
The origin of the word "robot"

The word "robot" comes from the Czech word robota, meaning "forced labor" or "drudgery." It was first used in 1920 in the play R.U.R. (Rossum's Universal Robots) by writer Karel Capek.

Fun Fact 2
The first industrial robot

Unimate, created in 1961 by George Devol and installed at a General Motors factory in New Jersey (USA), was the first industrial robot in history. It performed casting and welding tasks on cars.

Fun Fact 3
Robots in space

NASA's Opportunity rover, sent to Mars in 2004, was designed to operate for 90 days but functioned for an incredible 14 years, until 2018 — almost 56 times longer than expected.
