History of Robotics

From antiquity to modern robots: follow the fascinating evolution of robotics over the centuries.

The Evolution of Robotics

Humanity has dreamed of artificial beings for millennia. Here are the key milestones that brought us from ancient automatons to modern intelligent machines.

~250 BC

Ancient Automatons

Greek mathematicians and engineers already created automatic mechanisms. The philosopher Aristotle speculated about machines that could work on their own. Around 250 BC, Greek engineer Ctesibius invented an automatic water clock with moving figures. Hero of Alexandria later designed pneumatic and hydraulic automata for temples.

~1495

Leonardo da Vinci

Around 1495, Leonardo da Vinci designed an articulated mechanical knight capable of sitting, moving its arms, and turning its head. The blueprints were rediscovered only in the 1950s and successfully reconstructed in the 2000s. It is considered the first humanoid robot ever designed.

1738

Vaucanson's Automatons

French inventor Jacques de Vaucanson created remarkable automata: a flute player, a tabor pipe player, and a mechanical duck that simulated eating, digesting, and defecating. These machines astonished 18th-century Europe and were considered the pinnacle of mechanical engineering of the era.

1920

The Word "Robot" is Born

Czech writer Karel Capek premieres the play R.U.R. (Rossum's Universal Robots), using the Czech word robota (forced labor) for the first time to describe artificial workers. The term "robot" spread to all languages. Interestingly, it was Karel's brother Josef who actually coined the word.

1942

Asimov's Three Laws of Robotics

Science fiction writer Isaac Asimov publishes the story "Runaround," introducing the Three Laws of Robotics: (1) A robot may not injure a human being; (2) A robot must obey human orders; (3) A robot must protect its own existence. These laws continue to influence AI ethics debates today.

1954–1961

First Industrial Robot

George Devol patents the first programmable industrial robot (Unimate) in 1954. In 1961, Unimate is installed at a General Motors factory in Ewing Township, New Jersey, performing die casting and welding tasks. The era of industrial robotics had officially begun.

1966–1979

Mobile Robots and Computing

Shakey (1966–1972), developed at the Stanford Research Institute, was the first general-purpose mobile robot capable of reasoning about its actions. As computers became cheaper in the 1970s, robots started receiving embedded processors, gaining significantly more autonomy and intelligence.

1997

Mars and the Internet

NASA's Sojourner rover lands on Mars in 1997, becoming the first robotic vehicle to explore another planet. In the same period, the rise of the internet allowed robots to be monitored and controlled remotely over networks for the first time, opening up entirely new possibilities.

2000s

Humanoid Robots Emerge

Honda launches ASIMO in 2000, a humanoid robot capable of walking, climbing stairs, and recognizing faces. Boston Dynamics develops BigDog (2005), then Atlas. The Da Vinci Surgical System (2000) brings precision robotics into operating rooms. Robotics begins leaving the factory floor and entering the real world.

2010s

AI Meets Robotics

The fusion of Artificial Intelligence with robotics transforms the field. Self-driving cars (Tesla, Waymo), delivery drones (Amazon Prime Air), robotic surgery (Da Vinci), and personal assistants (Alexa, Siri) enter everyday life. Deep learning enables robots to see, hear, and understand language.

Today

Industry 4.0

We live in Industry 4.0, where collaborative robots (cobots), digital twins, IoT, and AI work together seamlessly. SpaceX uses robots to manufacture rockets. Tesla runs nearly fully automated factories. Amazon operates 750,000+ robots in its warehouses. The future of robotics has already arrived.

Important Milestones

Did You Know?
Isaac Asimov's Legacy

Isaac Asimov wrote more than 500 books and is considered the literary father of robotics. His Three Laws of Robotics are still studied today in AI ethics courses at universities worldwide, influencing real policy discussions about autonomous systems.

Did You Know?
The Word "Cyborg"

The word Cyborg (cybernetic organism) was created in 1960 by researchers Manfred Clynes and Nathan Kline to describe humans augmented with mechanical parts. Today, people with cochlear implants, pacemakers, or bionic limbs are technically considered cyborgs.

Did You Know?
Boston Dynamics Atlas

The Atlas robot from Boston Dynamics, first unveiled in 2013, weighs 80 kg and can do backflips, run, jump, and even dance — capabilities that seemed impossible just 20 years ago. It uses hydraulic actuators and real-time balance control algorithms.

Antikythera Mechanism (~100 BC): Discovered in a Greek shipwreck in 1901, this ancient device is considered the world's first analog computer. It tracked astronomical positions and dates with remarkable precision — 2,000 years before the Renaissance.

Industrial Revolution (1760–1840): The mechanization of production during the Industrial Revolution laid the groundwork for robotics. Steam-powered looms, mechanical presses, and early assembly lines were the precursors to today's automated factories.
