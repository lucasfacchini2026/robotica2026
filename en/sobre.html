Fundamentals

What is Robotics?

Understand the principles, components, and importance of robotics in modern society.

Definition

Complete Definition

Robotics is an interdisciplinary field of science and engineering dedicated to the design, construction, programming, and use of robots. It combines three main areas: mechanics (structure and movement), electronics (circuits and sensors), and programming (logic and algorithms).

The goal of robotics is to create machines that can perform tasks in the real world, either with or without direct human supervision. These tasks can be simple (moving objects on a conveyor belt) or extremely complex (performing heart surgery or exploring Mars).

Origin of the word: The word "robot" comes from the Czech robota, meaning "forced labor." It was coined by writer Karel Capek in his 1920 play R.U.R. (Rossum's Universal Robots), where artificial workers rebel against their creators.

IEEE Definition: A robot is a mechanical, electrical, or software system capable of perceiving its environment, making decisions, and performing tasks autonomously or semi-autonomously to achieve a goal.

Functioning

How Does a Robot Work?

Every robot, regardless of its size or complexity, follows the same basic cycle of operation.

Sensors

Sensors are the robot's "senses." They detect data from the environment: cameras (vision), LiDAR (distance), gyroscopes (balance), temperature sensors, microphones, and more. Without sensors, the robot is blind to the world around it.

Controller (Brain)

The microcontroller or computer processes sensor data and decides what to do. Modern robots use AI algorithms — including machine learning — that allow them to improve over time and handle unforeseen situations.

Actuators

Actuators are the robot's "muscles": electric motors, hydraulic cylinders, pneumatic pistons, and grippers that execute physical movement. Their precision and strength determine what kinds of tasks the robot can perform.

The Three Pillars

Mechanics + Electronics + Programming

Mechanics

The physical structure of a robot: its body, joints, links, and materials. Robotics engineers choose materials like steel, aluminum, and carbon fiber for their strength-to-weight ratio. Gears, bearings, and joints define the range of motion.

  • Rigid and flexible structures
  • Degrees of freedom (DoF)
  • Kinematic chains and linkages
Electronics

Electronic circuits power the robot, connect sensors to the controller, drive motors, and enable wireless communication (WiFi, Bluetooth, 5G). Without reliable electronics, the robot cannot perceive or act in the world.

  • Microcontrollers (Arduino, Raspberry Pi)
  • Power management (batteries, BMS)
  • Embedded communication modules
Programming

Software defines the robot's behavior: how it interprets sensor data, plans paths, avoids obstacles, and carries out tasks. AI and machine learning are increasingly central to modern robotics programming.

  • Languages: C++, Python, Java
  • ROS (Robot Operating System)
  • Computer vision and deep learning
Did You Know?

The most widely used operating system in robotics is ROS (Robot Operating System), developed at Stanford University in 2007. It is free, open-source, and used by researchers and companies worldwide — from university labs to Mars rovers.
